# Project scope

## Core research question

To what extent is economic inequality associated with lower generalised social trust and greater perceived social conflict across European countries
and over time?

## Secondary question

Are these relationships stronger in country-periods characterised by more divisive political discourse?

The secondary question remains contingent on identifying a theoretically defensible and empirically feasible measure of political discourse.

## Primary explanatory factor

Country-level economic inequality.

## Potential outcomes

1. Generalised social trust
2. Perceived social conflict

These will initially be treated as distinct dimensions rather than combined into a single social-cohesion index.

## Proposed analytical structure

Individuals nested within country-periods, using repeated cross-national survey data combined with contextual country-level indicators.

## Intended contribution

The project will examine whether inequality is related not only to lower interpersonal trust, but also to stronger perceptions of conflict between social groups. It will assess whether these outcomes represent distinct ways in which inequality may undermine social cohesion.

## Provisional geographical scope

European countries

## Deliberately outside the initial scope

- Establishing a definitive causal effect of inequality
- Developing a comprehensive index of social cohesion
- Explaining all dimensions of political polarisation
- Comparing Europe systematically with other world regions
- Treating political discourse as essential if suitable measures are unavailable

## Open decisions

- Can both outcomes can be addressed convincingly in one article?
- Can the design can identify within-country change as well as
  cross-country differences?
- Which dataset provides the strongest outcome measures and coverage?
- Is political discourse feasible as a moderator?
